XBT Coinbase Maturity: PR Analysis

bitcoinknots/bitcoin · compiled 23 Oct 2026 · 17 PRs analyzed

Strategic overview: lost among all these PRs?

The short version. Of the 17 PRs, #468 still has the most review and testing, but it changed on 23 Oct: the #477 commit was taken back out, so it again lets the earlier 45-day window unlock all at once at block 979,920. The options that avoid that one-block release are #477 (now a separate PR again), #478, #479 and the new #480 (permanent rolling 45 days). Numbers below are my reading of the PR threads as of the night of 23 Oct 2026 (US Central), not an official result.

The problem in three lines

  • #419 (merged 21 Sep) made coinbase rewards unspendable for 6,480 blocks (~45 days) from block 973,440. That lock ends at block 979,920, after which the rule goes back to 100 blocks.
  • The cliff: every reward from 973,440 to 979,919 would unlock together, about 20,000 BTC in one block. Many people want to avoid that; others say changing how already-mined coins unlock is itself the problem.
  • The length: is 45 days enough, or should it be 180 days or a year? And should a longer lock come with withholding payment from non-miner (stratum) blocks? That last step is in no PR’s code; #468’s author says the PR does not void anything.

Where things stand

  • #468 is at head 8a4ca1b (13 commits). On 23 Oct the author restored the exact tree of the release-candidate state (3395470, the one kwsantiago already tested) with one revert commit, which removes #477. He then added a test fix, a fix so getdeploymentinfo reports the 6,480-block rule before activation, and a wallet change that skips the cs_main lock for fully mature coinbase. His stated reasons: most ACKs predated #477, some became NACKs, changing the maturity of already-mined coins has never been done, and the release-candidate tree keeps the deadline within reach.
  • Two NACKs on #468 now. FlyTheElephant1 NACKs unless it contains #477, #478, #479 or similar code. luke-jr gives a Concept NACK: he thinks it accomplishes little for the emergency, releases the stratum-period rewards early, and locks the network longer than the original plan.
  • #480 is the newest alternative. It is smaller than #468, has no 180-day step and keeps the rule permanent. It has no formal reviews yet; its first head failed one existing unit test and was fixed twice within a few hours.
  • The deadline is about a week away (block 979,920), so the choice has to settle soon.

What is still open

  • Whether to change coins already mined. One side says the 979,920 release was part of the rule that activated, so changing it is retroactive. The other says it was always meant to be 45 days per coin and the one-block release is a flaw.
  • 180 days vs 1 year. A longer lock raises more risk of hashrate leaving; reviewers disagree on whether that means “slow” or “stalled”.
  • Voiding stratum rewards is a separate question that no PR implements. Some reviewers ACK #468 only on that basis; others want it as the point of the lock.
  • Days vs blocks. A block-count lock runs shorter than its day label when blocks come faster than 10 minutes.
  • The rolling window past RDTS expiry is not extended by the code; extending it would be a later decision. A permanent 45-day rule (#480) would need a hard fork to loosen, which #468’s author cites as a reason against enshrining it.

How you can help

  • Test it: check out the PR you support at its current head (8a4ca1b for #468, b525aec for #480 at the time of writing), build, run the unit tests plus the maturity functional tests, then post a “Tested ACK” with the commit hash and what you ran. Confirm the head first with git ls-remote https://github.com/bitcoinknots/bitcoin.git refs/pull/468/head.
  • Re-ACK or clarify if you approved #468 before 23 Oct: the version you tested may be the one with #477.
  • Say what you did. Hash, platform, tests, and anything that failed (for example, two unrelated functional tests fail on the base branch too).

#419 T.Softfork: Long coinbase maturity (part 1 of 3)

Merged 21 Sep 2026luke-jr29.x-knots

What it does. Enforces a 6,480-block (~45 day) maturity on coinbases from block 973,440, releasing at spending height 979,920, after which consensus returns to 100. Policy shows a flat 45-day maturity independent of consensus. Ships an extensible revalidation framework so nodes upgrading from an invalid chain rewind and recheck. Stated as step one of three: 45 days, then one year (RDTS expiry), then withholding rewards for blocks produced by non-miners (stratum-to-pool).

  • Review found real bugs: mempool PreChecks hardcodes a start height of 0, so every coinbase is held to 6,480 in relay even where consensus requires only 100 (raised by Kilombino and jasonsopko; unanswered in the thread I read).
  • Wallet accounting: f4u57ox reproduced a phantom 50 BTC immature credit plus a negative legacy balance; jasonsopko later confirmed the immature-credit part fixed.
  • Late upgrades and pruned nodes: fixed via atomic demotion and rewind; a snapshot-plus-background-validation edge case remains, judged irrelevant for mainnet (bases at 840k, 880k, 910k).
  • Schedule pressure: reviewers flagged under two days between release candidate and enforcement; the schedule shifted from 973,728 to 973,440, and pre-973,440 coinbases are no longer covered.

#467 Keep the 6480-block (~45-day) rolling coinbase maturity until RDTS expiry

RollingOpenalphaminetechopened 19 Oct4 commits

What it does. Merged #419 drops back to 100 at block 979,920. This keeps 6,480 blocks on a rolling basis: any coinbase mined from 973,440 needs 6,480 confirmations while the spending block's parent MTP is before RDTS expiry (1819756800, 2027-09-01). Each reward unlocks about 45 days after it is mined. After expiry everything returns to 100, with no extra six-week wait for rewards mined just before expiry.

  • Soft fork relative to #419: from 979,920 to expiry it only restricts #419's valid-block set.
  • Mempool and wallet code unchanged from #419, so policy stays stricter than consensus after expiry.
  • Heavy testing claimed: 35 focused unit cases, 27 functional runs, 16 mutants all caught. GUI, BDB, ZMQ and full lint not covered.
  • A node that ran #429 or #434 must reindex.

#468 T.Softfork: consensus: 180-day rolling coinbase maturity from height 979919 until RDTS expiry

RollingOpenBitcoinMechanicopened 20 Oct13 commits at head 8a4ca1b+779 −62, 32 fileslabel: L1 protocol change101 comments, 20 participants4 approvals, no conflicts

What it does. Alternative to #467. Rewards mined at height 979,919 or later with a parent MTP before expiry (1 Sep 2027) need 25,920 blocks (~180 days). A reward mined before expiry must serve its full period even if that lands after expiry; rewards mined at or after expiry return to 100. The first covered reward (979,919) is spendable at 1,005,839. The author expects the rolling window to continue, with its length adjustable by network health, but the code does not extend coverage past RDTS expiry by itself.

  • #477 was removed on 23 Oct. It had been added on 22 Oct as one reversible commit. Commit 24cbd56935 now restores the exact tree of 3395470f51, the release-candidate state that kwsantiago had already tested (rc2), without rewriting history. That drops #477’s 45-day extension for the old window, its policy correction and the rolling_coinbase_maturity_45d marker. The independent DNS test fix (1ef1119d1f) stays out of the PR on purpose.
  • The author’s reasons: most ACKs came before #477 was added, some became NACKs afterwards, and changing the maturity of already-mined coins “has never been done” and is best avoided. He notes that default mempool policy already holds those coins for 6,480 blocks, with weaker guarantees, and that the release-candidate tree gives a good chance of meeting the deadline without moving the start height. Earlier ACKers are asked to re-ACK, or to be counted as still ACK if they did not comment after #477 was added.
  • What this means for the old window: rewards mined from 973,440 to 979,918 keep #419’s rule through spending height 979,919 and then need only 100 blocks. Those mined up to 979,820 become spendable together at 979,920 (about 19,900 BTC by this page’s calculator; jasonsopko counts about 20,260 BTC in the whole window). The last ~100 blocks follow at 100 blocks each. The 180-day depth is not applied retroactively.
  • Commits after the revert: c414307229 (test-only: the crash test resubmits a block a crashed node never received), 0237da5824 (getdeploymentinfo describes the historical 6,480-block rule before rolling activation instead of showing it inactive, as kwsantiago found), and 8a4ca1b223 (the wallet skips the block lookup and cs_main lock for rewards deeper than the largest policy maturity). jasonsopko writes that no consensus or relay code changed in the last two.
  • Uses rolling_coinbase_maturity_180d_v2 and keeps #419’s marker for the historical rule. Bundles a crash-recovery fix (ordered FlushStateToDisk with FORCE_SYNC, following kwsantiago’s approach in #441).
  • Author’s stated intent: a long window gives time to organize retribution against malicious miners, but the PR does not itself void anything and support for it should not be read as endorsing future voiding. For next summer he suggests continuing the 180-day rolling rule if things look healthy; he argues against falling back to 45 days or making 45 days permanent, because that would need a hard fork to undo.
  • luke-jr (Concept NACK): accomplishes nothing for the emergency, keeps the network in “emergency mode”, releases the stratum-period rewards early, and locks the network six months longer than the original one-year plan. He prefers letting the long maturity expire after 45 days. He has not reviewed the code and discloses that the existing lock causes problems for CONVOY. He adds that if the community wants this, Knots has no choice but to merge it. chrisguida (still Concept ACK) would rather delay activation, which the design allows, than rush; kwsantiago (still Concept ACK) prefers letting the current rule expire and planning a proper softfork.

#469 Continue RDTS, with a rolling 40320-block (~280-day) coinbase maturity (builds on #467)

RollingOpenZERODAYKEKopened 20 Octlabel: L1 protocol change (luke-jr, 22 Oct)

What it does. Two mainnet parameter changes on top of #467. First, RdtsExpiryTime becomes INT64_MAX, so BIP-110 / RDTS never expires. #479 T.Softfork Hybrid 100 band on knots

RollingOpenFlyTheElephant1opened 22 Oct7 commits, 28 files, +670 −46base: 29.x-knots2 participants

What it does. A hybrid of the #468 approach. Three zones: (1) coins created in #419’s original window (973,440 to 979,919) get 180-day (25,920-block) maturity, so there is no cliff at 979,920; (2) the next 6,480 blocks (979,920 to 986,399) use ordinary 100-block maturity, a short grace window so miners can be paid over about 45 days; (3) from 986,400 the 180-day rolling rule resumes, still contingent on parent MTP before RDTS expiry. Depends on and includes #468 (six of the seven commits are #468’s; the new one is “consensus: 100-block band between historical 180-day and resumed 180-day”, verified).

  • Stated rationale: miners claim they assumed the initial 45 days would all mature at once, so the band lets them be paid as they expect. The post says this pays them over a 45-day period.
  • Tests (src/test/coinbase_maturity_tests.cpp): 973,440 and 979,919 require 25,920; 979,920 and 986,399 require only 100; 986,400 requires 25,920. The maturity tests report no errors. Merge status: no conflicts.
  • Derived timeline (my arithmetic, 10-minute blocks). Band coins mature at about 979,920 to 986,499. Old-window coins mature from 999,360 to 1,005,839. Coins from 986,400 mature from 1,012,320. So nothing matures for about 12,860 blocks (89 days) after the band, and again for about 6,480 blocks (45 days) after the old window. This is not unique to #479: any switch from a 45-day to a 180-day lock leaves a stretch with no unlocks. Under #468 as it stands (no #477) the stretch after the old window’s one-block release is about 25,800 blocks (about 179 days), from 980,018 to 1,005,839; with #477 it was about 19,440 blocks (135 days), from 986,398. #479 splits its stretch into two shorter gaps of about 19,340 blocks in total.
  • The author’s framing is by era, not payout method: the 973,440 to 979,919 window (“the stratum era”) stays locked, and the band is the “carrot” (“a prisoner exchange”). But everything mined inside the band is paid at 100 blocks, DATUM or SV1, so it does not separate them going forward. The old window is re-locked to 180 days, so the retroactivity objection raised on #476, #477 and #478 applies to it too.

#480 45 Day Maturity Softfork - Permanent

RollingOpenFlyTheElephant1opened 23 Oct1 commit, 2 fileshead b525aec (earlier heads 235347e, 98f8ea8, eb6e889, c705c3a, 4bd9f45)base: 29.x-knots

What it does. Makes #419’s 6,480-block maturity permanent from height 973,440. On mainnet it sets CoinbaseMaturityLongReleaseHeight to INT_MAX and CoinbaseMaturityLong to 6,480, and extends the revalidation marker to the same range so nodes that certified the temporary window re-check it. Every coin from 973,440 then unlocks 6,480 blocks after it is mined, with no release at 979,920, no step to 180 days and no expiry. Coins mined from 973,441 to 979,919 therefore unlock one at a time, at their own height + 6,480, instead of all at once. It stands alone on the 29.x-knots base; the author says other rules can be layered above this floor.

  • Test history. The first head (235347e) failed an existing unit test, validation_chainstatemanager_tests, because it also moved the shared revalidation helper’s stop_height by one block (103 instead of 102 on regtest), which the PR’s own test file did not cover. Geobees-Veloni reported it and showed that reverting that line fixes it. The author reverted the helper in 98f8ea8 but left one testnet4 assertion unchanged, then fixed that in eb6e889.
  • eb6e889: in Geobees-Veloni’s run (Ubuntu on WSL2, GCC 15.2, no GUI) the build succeeds, coinbase_maturity_tests and validation_chainstatemanager_tests pass, the unit suite passes 146/146, and feature_chainstate_revalidation, mempool_long_coinbase_maturity, mempool_spend_coinbase and rpc_blockchain pass. One nit: with the release height at INT_MAX, rpc/blockchain.cpp returned early, so the long-maturity entry in getdeploymentinfo would not be reported on mainnet.
  • c705c3a and 4bd9f45: the author then removed the RPC early return. At c705c3a that made rpc_blockchain.py fail on both transports, because networks that never configure the deployment, such as regtest, reported a long_coinbase_maturity entry (height 2147483647, maturity 100, inactive) where the test expects none.   • Current head b525aec: in the same environment (Ubuntu on WSL2, GCC 15.2, no GUI) the build succeeds, the unit suite passes 146/146 (including coinbase_maturity_tests and validation_chainstatemanager_tests), and rpc_blockchain (both transports), feature_chainstate_revalidation, mempool_long_coinbase_maturity and mempool_spend_coinbase pass. It edits the same constants as #468, so combining them would likely conflict (not tested).

What the thread tells us

  • Blocks are not days here. Reviewers measured 107 to 224 s average spacing in September, so any block-count lock runs well short of its "calendar" label. MTP-based rules (#403, #429 policy, #434 last period) avoid that; #467 to #469 mix block-count depth with MTP expiry.
  • Direction of change matters. Lengthening a lock stays a soft fork; shortening is a hard fork (kwsantiago on #403). Starting long is hard to reverse.
  • The next flag day is block 979,920. #429, #434, #467, #468 and #469 all diverge from #419 from there; nodes must upgrade before it or rewind later (pruned nodes may need a reindex).
  • Policy versus consensus. #419's mempool holds every coinbase to 6,480, even where consensus allows 100; #467 and #469 keep that on purpose, #468 narrows it to covered rewards.
  • Retroactivity. #476, #477, #478 and #480 change when rewards already mined under the 6,480 rule unlock; #476 and #477 each draw a NACK from the same reviewer (SeventyEi8ht), and #478 and #480 have no such comments yet. #468 avoids this by starting at 979,919 and, since 23 Oct, no longer includes #477; #467 and #469 only extend the lock on coverage #419 already applies. The cost of #468's approach is the cliff: about 20,000 BTC unlocking in one block at 979,920. FlyTheElephant1 NACKs #468 for exactly that reason; BitcoinMechanic replies that those coins are released by the existing code and that most reviewers do not want it changed.
  • Who bears the lock. Measured on #404: pools that pay hashers on chain within about 100 blocks (AlphaPool, about 48% of spent value in the sample) pass the lock straight to hashers, so a longer lock mostly hits hasher paychecks, and a small-output exemption does not fix it.
  • Smoothing the release. #434 uses staged dates, #404 batches by height, #467 to #469 and #476 roll per reward, and #409 randomizes by block hash. Critics of #409 raise grinding and pool consolidation; critics of long locks generally raise liquidity and the advantage to large pools.
  • Real fault line: whether the lock is a reorg-safety or deterrent tool (45 days, rolling) or a lever toward the retribution step (180 days to a year). The NACKs and the conditional mynpub comment all sit on that line.
